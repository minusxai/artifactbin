## Read first

A brief is eight to twelve lines: reader, job, facts, priority, page type,
system, thesis, composition, acceptance, unknowns. The thesis names the
organizing idea, the object the hand draws and the device that carries the
data. The rejected line is what teaches: the obvious composition and why it
fails this reader. Read at most one brief per task, and only when the job is
unfamiliar; never copy a brief's system or object into a different subject.

## The briefs

### Dashboard · allocate attention

Reader: a support lead beginning a shift. Facts: 180 open, 36 overdue, 15
agents; overdue is a subset. Priority: Technical holds half the overdue.
System: `system-volta.md`, one loud number on quiet paper. Thesis: a shift
briefing whose diagnosis leads into a queue comparison and an inspectable
ledger; the split bar carries the overdue share. Composition: diagnosis →
comparison → filtered detail; mobile keeps that order. Rejected: a tile grid
of eight KPIs that shows everything and prioritizes nothing. Acceptance:
the queue filter updates every derived number; sort works; no clipped labels.

### Editorial · understand evidence (restrained)

Reader: a market committee deciding whether to continue an earlier-opening
pilot. Facts: six Sundays, 510 transactions, 42 survey responses, 26
preferring the earlier hour, $1,800 extra staffing. System:
`system-dossier.md`, paper narrates and ink proves. Thesis: a pilot report
that states what the count proves and what it cannot, with every percentage
naming its denominator; the facts rail carries the numbers. Composition:
findings up front → numbered claims → method footer. Rejected: an
illustrated feature with invented voices; the committee wants the evidence,
not the atmosphere. Acceptance: transactions never read as shoppers.

### Plan · execute a change (restrained)

Reader: a coding agent, then a reviewer. Facts: a document-approval flow in a
TypeScript app; authors cannot approve their own work; an approval must
never attach to stale content. System: `system-drafting.md`, every line a
measurement. Thesis: an engineering drawing set where the race invariant
leads as a drawing, then contracts, then evidence-aware tasks. Composition:
invariant → wireframes → flow → ledger. Rejected: a task timeline with a
component inventory; it executes nothing. Acceptance: discovery, implementation
and verified results are distinguished; every task starts pending.

### Deck · make a decision

Reader: a volunteer cinema board approving a projector. Facts: 1,280 seats
offered, 960 admissions, three late starts from faults, $32,000 to replace.
System: `system-redline.md`, one word then the number. Thesis: a decision
programme where each slide is its own composition and the motion is the last
slide; the big-number slide carries the late starts. Composition: experience
→ evidence → options → cost → unknowns → motion. Rejected: a cover, a
contents slide and six identical chart slides. Acceptance: admissions never
read as unique people; no invented payback.

### App · complete a workflow

Reader: a maker booking a machine. Facts: three tools, three slots, one
already taken. System: `system-signout.md`, the sheet on the wall made
clickable. Thesis: availability is the primary surface, the selection stays
visible, confirmation becomes a booking record. Composition: sheet → review
→ record, with unavailable, empty and success states drawn. Rejected: a
form with a submit button and a toast. Acceptance: the taken slot stays
taken; back navigation keeps the selection; refresh behaviour is stated.

### Landing · nothing fits

Reader: a first-time visitor to a ceramics exhibition. Facts: three
weekends, free admission, three paid workshops. The user supplies a brand:
unglazed clay, one cobalt, a stencil face no system carries. No listed
system fits: the nearest, `system-almanac.md`, loses the stencil and the
cobalt. Thesis: an invitation whose opening is one drawn kiln with the dates,
the price and the next action composed into it; the schedule is the second
surface. Composition: invitation → programme → practicalities. Resolution:
say so in the catalogue's four lines, offer the nearest, and on approval
write a one-artifact system in the artifact's Helmet. Acceptance: the
primary action reaches the schedule; the demo is labelled as one.
