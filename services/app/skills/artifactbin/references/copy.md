## Read first

Write what the reader needs to know or do. Keep the design expressive and
the language clear. Apply these rules to reports, slides, captions and UI
copy unless the user requests a different voice or supplies exact wording.

Use [ASD-STE100](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_faq.html) as a reference for
clarity. This guide adopts selected principles; it does not enforce the
standard's controlled dictionary or claim formal STE compliance.

## Sentences and paragraphs

- Prefer familiar words, concrete nouns and active verbs. Name the actor
  when it matters. Use the same term for the same thing throughout.
- Keep one main idea per sentence. As editing targets, use at most 20 words
  for instructions and 25 for descriptions. Put one action in each step.
- Start each paragraph with its point. Usually use 1–3 sentences and about
  60 words or fewer. Split a paragraph when the topic changes.
- Use a list for steps or parallel items, and a table for comparisons.
  Keep connected explanations in prose. Do not turn every sentence into a bullet.
- Put the result first and supporting detail after it. Move long methods
  or reference material to a clearly labelled section. Do not shrink type
  to fit a text blob on a slide or card.
- Preserve quantities, units, sources, uncertainty, conditions and exceptions.
  Brevity must not change the claim. Keep required wording and quotations intact.

## Remove AI filler

- Delete empty openings and transitions: “In today's fast-paced world”,
  “It's worth noting”, “Let's delve into” and “In conclusion”. State the point.
- Replace vague hype such as “seamless”, “game-changing”, “robust” and
  “unlock the power” with the specific capability or evidence. A technical
  term is fine when its precise meaning is needed.
- Avoid decorative **contrastive negation**: “It's not X; it's Y”, “Not
  just X, but Y”, “X, not Y” and “More than X”. State the claim directly.
  Use contrast only to correct a real misconception or explain a relevant
  difference. Related devices include antithesis (opposing ideas) and
  correctio or epanorthosis (an emphatic correction).
- Cut question-and-answer headings, invented jargon and decorative metaphors.
- Remove summaries that merely repeat the paragraph, caption or headline.
  Each sentence must add a fact, explanation, qualification or action.

Before: “Leverage our seamless workflow to unlock powerful insights.”
After: “Filter the table by region to compare sales.”

Before: “This isn't a dashboard. It's your command center.”
After: “Track orders and resolve delays from one dashboard.”

Before: “It is important to note that the upload was unsuccessful.”
After: “The upload failed. Choose a CSV file smaller than 10 MB.”
Use that limit only if the product actually enforces it.

## Final pass

Read only the headings: they should explain the document's argument or tasks.
Then remove repeated claims, split dense paragraphs and replace vague words.
Check that the shorter copy still preserves the evidence and its limits.
